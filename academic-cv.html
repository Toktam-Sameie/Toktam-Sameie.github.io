Research profile

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at the University of California, Riverside, advised by Prof. Erfan Nozari. My research lies at the intersection of computational neuroscience, neural population coding, and machine learning.

I am interested in understanding how neural populations represent, integrate, and transmit information across space, time, and brain regions. I use neural decoding, mathematical modeling, information theory, and large-scale neural recordings to investigate how the statistical and dynamical properties of neural activity shape what can be decoded and at which spatiotemporal scales.

My work has identified mesoscale optimality in neural decoding and bracket coding in sensory population activity. More recently, I have been developing adaptive temporal optimization methods and Transformer-based models to study evolving neural timescales, cross-region information, and candidate neuronal interactions. I am also interested in translating these methods into interpretable machine learning and neural engineering applications.

Research contributions and ongoing projects

Graduate Research Assistant, UC Riverside · September 2022–present
Advisor: Prof. Erfan Nozari

  • Spatiotemporal resolution & NeuroPixelHD

    Identified mesoscale decoding optimality in large-scale neural recordings and developed a hyperdimensional encoding–decoding model to control spatial and temporal aggregation. [Code]

  • Bracket coding

    Developed sweeping-integration methods for detecting temporally bounded rate-coded intervals and synchronized population boundaries. Evaluated decoding, cross-region synchrony, and LFP phase locking against randomized controls. [Code]

  • Dynamic temporal optimization & spike Transformers

    Developed Integration–Split, a decoding-guided adaptive temporal segmentation algorithm, and a PyTorch Transformer with spike-event, neuron-identity and continuous-time embeddings. Compared attention structure with decoding-optimal scales using ablations and randomized controls. [Code]

  • Multi-region neural decoding

    Compared region-specific self-attention, cross-attention, linear fusion, logistic-regression baselines and partial information decomposition to study complementary versus synergistic information. Ongoing research.

  • Directed interactions in spiking networks

    Developing spike-to-spike predictive Transformers for candidate connectivity inference in simulated excitatory/inhibitory networks. Evaluating known ground-truth connectivity and predictive ablations; attention alone does not establish causality. Ongoing research.

  • Intrinsic neuronal timescales

    Investigating relationships between single-neuron autocorrelation timescales and population decoding-optimal temporal windows. Ongoing research.

  • Multimodal emotion recognition

    Applied deep canonical correlation analysis to EEG and eye-movement data to learn shared representations across modalities.

  • Ultrasound-based light guiding

    Investigated ultrasound-induced refractive-index modulation for guiding light through scattering tissue, with potential applications in non-invasive neurotechnology.
