About me

I am a fifth-year PhD candidate in Mechanical Engineering at UC Riverside, advised by Prof. Erfan Nozari. My work bridges computational neuroscience and machine learning, with a focus on neural population coding, temporal dynamics, and interpretable data-driven models. I develop methods for decoding large-scale neural recordings and studying the structure of information within and across brain regions.

My interests also include machine learning research and engineering, neural time-series modeling, NeuroAI, neural signal processing, and computational neurotechnology.

Research themes

Spatiotemporal neural coding

When do spatial and temporal integration help or hinder the decoding of population activity? Investigating mesoscale optimality and the coexistence of rate and temporal coding.

Dynamic timescales & neural information

Understanding how the decoding-optimal temporal scale changes during stimulus processing and how signal and noise autocorrelation shape temporal integration.

Deep learning for neural data

Using Transformers and spike-event models to decode stimuli, characterize temporal organization, and analyze representational dynamics.

Networks & cross-region information

Comparing information contributed by multiple recorded brain regions and exploring predictive models for directed interactions in simulated neural circuits.

Selected research & engineering projects

Dynamic optimal temporal resolution & Transformer decoding

Developed Integration–Split, a decoding-guided adaptive temporal segmentation approach; examined evolving neural information and the temporal structure learned by a spike-event Transformer. Evaluated attention-head ablations and randomized controls.

Mesoscale neural decoding & NeuroPixelHD

Studied the impact of spatiotemporal resolution on stimulus decodability and developed a hyperdimensional encoding–decoding framework for multi-unit activity.

Bracket coding in visual population activity

Developed sweeping-integration analyses to study temporally bounded rate-coded intervals and synchronized population boundaries. Studied thalamocortical visual hierarchy, LFP phase locking, and statistical controls on Allen and IBL recordings.

Cross-region information & neural Transformers

Compared region-specific Transformer encoders, cross-attention, linear fusion, logistic regression, and partial information decomposition to assess complementary and synergistic information in neural recordings. Ongoing research.

Deep learningInformation theory

Directed network inference from spikes

Developing spike-to-spike predictive Transformer models and evaluating candidate interactions against known connections in simulated excitatory/inhibitory neural networks, including predictive ablations. Ongoing research; predictive attention is not treated as proof of causality.

Intrinsic neuronal and population timescales

Investigating how timescales derived from single-neuron autocorrelations relate to the temporal resolution that optimizes population decoding. Ongoing research.
