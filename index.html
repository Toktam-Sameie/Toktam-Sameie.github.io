About me

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at the University of California, Riverside, advised by Prof. Erfan Nozari. My research lies at the intersection of computational neuroscience, neural population coding, and machine learning.

I am interested in understanding how neural populations represent, integrate, and transmit information across space, time, and brain regions. I use neural decoding, mathematical modeling, information theory, and large-scale neural recordings to investigate how the statistical and dynamical properties of neural activity shape what can be decoded and at which spatiotemporal scales.

My work has identified mesoscale optimality in neural decoding and bracket coding in sensory population activity. More recently, I have been developing adaptive temporal optimization methods and Transformer-based models to study evolving neural timescales, cross-region information, and candidate neuronal interactions. I am also interested in translating these methods into interpretable machine learning and neural engineering applications.

I welcome conversations about research collaborations and opportunities in computational neuroscience, neural engineering, NeuroAI, and machine learning.

Research & technical projects

  • At what scales is neural information best decoded?

    Found that visual information is decoded most accurately at intermediate spatial and temporal resolutions. Developed NeuroPixelHD, a hyperdimensional encoding–decoding framework for controlling spatial and temporal aggregation in large-scale neural recordings. [Paper] [Code]

  • How do rate and temporal coding coexist?

    Identified bracket coding: population activity is rate-coded within temporal intervals separated by precisely timed, synchronized boundaries. Developed sweeping-integration analyses and evaluated decoding, cross-region synchrony, and LFP phase locking against randomized controls. [Paper] [Code]

  • What determines the optimal decoding timescale?

    Contributed to a theoretical framework showing how the temporal autocorrelation of signal and noise determines when integration improves stimulus discriminability and when excessive averaging degrades it. [Paper]

  • Does the optimal neural decoding timescale change over time?

    Found that the decoding-optimal temporal scale becomes finer near peak stimulus information and subsequently re-expands. Developed Integration–Split, a decoding-guided adaptive segmentation algorithm, and a PyTorch spike-event Transformer whose learned temporal organization was evaluated with attention-head ablations and randomized controls. Examined associations with signal-to-noise structure and nonlinear predictive advantage. [Paper] [Code]

  • Do brain regions encode information synergistically?

    Compared region-specific Transformer encoders, cross-attention, linear fusion, logistic regression, and partial information decomposition. In the datasets studied, explicit cross-attention did not consistently improve decoding over independent regional representations, suggesting a largely complementary contribution. Ongoing research.

  • Can neural connectivity be inferred from spikes?

    Developing spike-to-spike predictive Transformers to identify candidate directed interactions in simulated excitatory/inhibitory networks. Evaluating inferred connections against known ground-truth connectivity and predictive ablations; attention alone does not establish causality. Ongoing research.

  • Do neuronal timescales predict population decoding scales?

    Investigating whether intrinsic timescales derived from single-neuron spike-train autocorrelation predict the temporal window that maximizes population decoding. Using controlled spiking-network simulations to examine the influence of neuronal and synaptic dynamics. Ongoing research.

  • Can brain and eye signals reveal emotions?

    Applied deep canonical correlation analysis to learn shared representations from EEG and eye-movement data for multimodal emotion recognition.

  • Can ultrasound guide light through tissue?

    Investigated ultrasound-induced refractive-index modulation for guiding light through scattering tissue, with potential applications in non-invasive neurotechnology.
